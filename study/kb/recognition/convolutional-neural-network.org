#+title: Convolutional neural networks and deep learning
#+summary: Deep learning stacks many layers that learn features directly from pixels; a CNN uses convolution layers (small learned kernels shared over the image), non-linearities and pooling to build increasingly abstract spatial–spectral features, and is used for scene classification, semantic segmentation (U-Net) and object detection in remote sensing.

* Construction
- *Convolution layer*: \(K\) kernels of size \(k\times k\times C_{in}\), each slid over the input like a [[id:moving-window-filters][moving-window filter]] but with learned weights, producing \(K\) feature maps; weight sharing makes the number of parameters independent of the image size.
- *Activation*: ReLU.
- *Pooling*: max or average over \(2\times2\) windows: downsampling, invariance to small shifts.
- *Head*: fully connected layers + softmax (scene/patch classification) or an upsampling decoder with skip connections (U-Net: pixel-wise maps).
Trained end to end by [[id:backpropagation][backpropagation]] on large labelled sets; transfer learning reuses networks pre-trained on other images; data augmentation (rotations, flips) compensates for few labels.

* Intuition
Instead of choosing texture measures and indices by hand, the network learns which local patterns matter, starting from edges and ending with fields, roofs and roads.

* Pitfall
Deep networks need many labels and generalise poorly to other sensors, regions and seasons than those they were trained on (domain shift); a high test accuracy on patches from the training scene says little about a new country.
