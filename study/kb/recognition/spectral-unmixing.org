#+title: Linear spectral unmixing
#+summary: A pixel larger than the objects it contains is a mixture: the linear mixing model writes its spectrum as \(\mathbf x=E\mathbf a+\mathbf n\), a combination of the endmember spectra \(E\) weighted by abundances \(a_j\ge0\), \(\sum a_j=1\); solving the constrained least-squares problem gives sub-pixel fractions instead of a single label.

* Method
With \(B\) bands and \(q\) endmembers (\(q\le B+1\)), \(E\in\mathbb R^{B\times q}\):
\[ \hat{\mathbf a}=\arg\min_{\mathbf a}\|\mathbf x-E\mathbf a\|^2\quad\text{s.t.}\quad a_j\ge0,\ \textstyle\sum_ja_j=1. \]
- unconstrained solution \(\hat{\mathbf a}=(E^\top E)^{-1}E^\top\mathbf x\);
- sum-to-one only: closed form with a Lagrange multiplier;
- fully constrained (FCLS): quadratic programming.
Endmembers come from libraries, field spectra, or the image itself (pure pixels at the vertices of the data cloud's simplex, e.g. N-FINDR, vertex component analysis). The residual \(\|\mathbf x-E\hat{\mathbf a}\|\) flags missing endmembers.

* Example
A 30 m pixel with 60% grass, 30% soil, 10% road: \(\mathbf x\approx0.6\,\mathbf e_{grass}+0.3\,\mathbf e_{soil}+0.1\,\mathbf e_{asphalt}\). Fraction maps of impervious surfaces or vegetation cover are produced this way from [[id:earth-observation-missions][Landsat]].

Mixed pixels lie inside the simplex of the endmembers; their barycentric coordinates are the abundances.

* Intuition
A pixel is a smoothie: knowing the taste of each fruit, the taste of the mix tells the proportions.

* Pitfall
Mixing is linear only when materials sit side by side; intimate mixtures (sand grains) and multiple scattering in canopies are non-linear, and endmember variability (many "grasses") blurs the estimates.
