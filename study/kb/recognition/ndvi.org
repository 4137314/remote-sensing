#+title: NDVI and normalised difference indices
#+summary: \(\mathrm{NDVI}=\dfrac{\rho_{NIR}-\rho_{red}}{\rho_{NIR}+\rho_{red}}\in[-1,1]\) exploits the contrast between red absorption by chlorophyll and high NIR reflectance of leaves: dense green vegetation gives 0.6–0.9, soil 0.1–0.2, water negative; the normalisation cancels much of the illumination variation.

* Definition
\[ \mathrm{NDVI}=\frac{\rho_{NIR}-\rho_{red}}{\rho_{NIR}+\rho_{red}}. \]
Same pattern for other surfaces: NDWI \(=(\rho_{green}-\rho_{NIR})/(\rho_{green}+\rho_{NIR})\) for open water, NDSI \(=(\rho_{green}-\rho_{SWIR})/(\rho_{green}+\rho_{SWIR})\) for snow, NBR (NIR, SWIR2) for burn severity.

* Derivation
A shadow or a slope multiplies both bands by the same factor \(k\): \((k\rho_N-k\rho_R)/(k\rho_N+k\rho_R)\) is unchanged. A ratio-based index is therefore robust to illumination, unlike the simple difference \(\rho_N-\rho_R\).

* Example
Sentinel-2: \(\mathrm{NDVI}=(B8-B4)/(B8+B4)\), on reflectances. NDVI time series track crop phenology: green-up, peak, senescence, harvest.

* Intuition
Vegetation is the only common surface that is dark red and bright infrared at the same time; NDVI measures exactly that contrast.

* Pitfall
NDVI saturates over dense canopies (it stops increasing beyond leaf area index ~3) and is sensitive to soil background in sparse vegetation; indices such as EVI and SAVI correct partially.
