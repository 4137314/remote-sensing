#+title: Recognition systems
#+summary: Recognition extracts information from the data — thematic maps, change maps, bio/geo-physical parameters, risk maps, 3-D models — either by photointerpretation or automatically; an automatic classifier chains pre-processing, feature extraction, a classifier trained on labelled samples (supervised) or not (unsupervised), and post-processing, and is designed in a cycle of feature selection, model selection, training and validation.

* Model
Architecture: scene → acquisition → pre-processing (corrections, registration) → feature extraction → classifier (with a supervisor providing training labels) → post-processing (majority filtering, merging) → thematic map.

Design cycle: data acquisition → feature selection → model selection (using prior knowledge) → classifier training → validation, iterating on any step whose results are inadequate.

- *Supervised*: training samples with known labels (ground truth from field surveys or photointerpretation).
- *Unsupervised*: no labels; the data are grouped into clusters ([[id:kmeans-clustering][clustering]]) and the analyst names them afterwards.
- *Photointerpretation vs automatic*: the human uses context and experience but is slow, qualitative and subjective; automatic recognition is fast, quantitative, objective, but cannot exploit all the perceptual knowledge of an expert.

* Intuition
A classifier is a learned rule from "what the pixel measures" to "what the ground is"; everything before it shapes what it can learn, everything after it cleans its answers.

* Pitfall
Training and validation samples must be different and independent: evaluating on the training pixels (or on neighbouring pixels of the same fields) overstates accuracy.
