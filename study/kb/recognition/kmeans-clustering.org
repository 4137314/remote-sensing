#+title: k-means clustering
#+summary: Unsupervised classification groups pixels without labels: k-means alternates assigning each pixel to the nearest of \(k\) centroids and moving each centroid to the mean of its pixels, monotonically decreasing the within-cluster sum of squares; the analyst then labels the spectral clusters as information classes.

* Algorithm
#+CAPTION: k-means: assign each sample to the nearest centre, move each centre to the mean of its samples, and repeat until the assignments stop changing.
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require feature vectors $\mathbf x_n$, number of clusters $k$
\State initialise centroids $\boldsymbol\mu_1,\dots,\boldsymbol\mu_k$ (random pixels or a grid in feature space)
\Repeat
\ForAll{$n$} \State $c_n\gets\arg\min_j\|\mathbf x_n-\boldsymbol\mu_j\|^2$ \EndFor
\ForAll{$j$} \State $\boldsymbol\mu_j\gets$ mean of $\{\mathbf x_n: c_n=j\}$ \EndFor
\Until{assignments do not change}
\State \Return $c$, $\boldsymbol\mu$
\end{algorithmic}
Objective \(J=\sum_n\|\mathbf x_n-\boldsymbol\mu_{c_n}\|^2\): each step does not increase it, so the algorithm converges, to a local minimum. ISODATA adds splitting of elongated clusters and merging of close ones, so \(k\) adapts.

* Example
#+begin_src python
  from sklearn.cluster import KMeans
  km = KMeans(n_clusters=8, n_init=10, random_state=0).fit(F)
  cluster_map = km.labels_.reshape(rows, cols)
#+end_src

* Intuition
Let the data say which groups exist; then a human decides what each group is on the ground.

* Pitfall
Clusters are spectral, not semantic: one class may split into several clusters and several classes may share one. Results depend on \(k\), initialisation and feature scaling.
