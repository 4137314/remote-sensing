#+title: Scattergram
#+summary: A scattergram plots, for two bands, the number of pixels at each pair of values (a two-dimensional histogram); it shows clusters, their correlation between bands and the classes' separability, and is the basic exploratory view of a multispectral image.

* Definition
\(S(i,j)=\#\{(y,x): I_{b_1}(y,x)=i,\ I_{b_2}(y,x)=j\}\), displayed as an image with logarithmic grey scale. Typical structures in the red–NIR plane: the *soil line* (bare soils from dark to bright along a diagonal), vegetation above it (high NIR, low red), water near the origin.

* Example
#+begin_src python
  H, xe, ye = np.histogram2d(red.ravel(), nir.ravel(), bins=256)
  plt.imshow(np.log1p(H.T), origin="lower", extent=[xe[0], xe[-1], ye[0], ye[-1]])
#+end_src
Selecting a region of the scattergram and highlighting the corresponding pixels in the image links [[id:feature-space][feature space]] and geography.

* Intuition
It is the image seen from inside the feature space: where the pixels pile up, there is a class.

* Pitfall
Highly correlated bands (visible bands among themselves) put all pixels on a narrow diagonal: little separability. Choose band pairs that carry different information, such as red and NIR.
