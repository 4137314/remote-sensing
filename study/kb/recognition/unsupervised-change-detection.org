#+title: Unsupervised change detection with EM and Bayes
#+summary: The difference (or log-ratio) image is modelled as a mixture of two classes, unchanged and changed, \(p(d)=P_np(d\mid\omega_n)+P_cp(d\mid\omega_c)\); the expectation–maximization algorithm estimates the priors and the class densities without any label, and the Bayes rule then gives the minimum-error threshold — optionally refined with spatial context.

* Method
1. Build \(d\): magnitude of the change vector (optical) or [[id:change-detection][log-ratio]] (SAR).
2. Model each class (Gaussian for differences of optical data; generalized Gaussian or Nakagami-type densities for SAR ratios).
3. *EM*, from an initial split (e.g. a rough threshold):
   - E-step: posterior of each pixel \(\displaystyle P(\omega_c\mid d_i)=\frac{P_cp(d_i\mid\omega_c)}{P_np(d_i\mid\omega_n)+P_cp(d_i\mid\omega_c)}\);
   - M-step: \(P_c=\frac1N\sum_iP(\omega_c\mid d_i)\), \(\mu_c=\frac{\sum_iP(\omega_c\mid d_i)d_i}{\sum_iP(\omega_c\mid d_i)}\), \(\sigma_c^2\) likewise; same for \(\omega_n\);
   - iterate until the parameters stop changing (the [[id:bayes-classifier][likelihood]] never decreases).
4. *Decision*: Bayes rule \(P_cp(d\mid\omega_c)>P_np(d\mid\omega_n)\), i.e. a threshold \(T\) where the weighted densities cross.
5. *[[id:photointerpretation][Context]]*: Markov random fields or majority filtering add the prior that changes form compact regions.

* Intuition
Without labels, let the histogram of differences explain itself as "a big hill of nothing happened" plus "a small hill of change", and cut where one becomes more likely than the other.

* Pitfall
EM finds a local optimum and assumes the chosen family of densities fits: a strongly non-Gaussian unchanged class (seasonal effects, misregistration) pulls the threshold and floods the map with false changes.
