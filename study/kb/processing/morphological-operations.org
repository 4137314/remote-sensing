#+title: Mathematical morphology
#+summary: With a small structuring element \(B\), erosion shrinks objects (a pixel stays only if \(B\) fits inside), dilation grows them, opening (erosion then dilation) removes objects and protrusions smaller than \(B\), closing (dilation then erosion) fills gaps and holes smaller than \(B\): the standard clean-up of binary masks and a source of shape features.

* Method
For a binary image \(X\) and structuring element \(B\) (e.g. a \(3\times3\) square or a disk):
- *erosion* \(X\ominus B=\{p: B_p\subseteq X\}\) — grey-level version: [[id:statistical-filters][minimum filter]];
- *dilation* \(X\oplus B=\{p: B_p\cap X\neq\emptyset\}\) — maximum filter;
- *opening* \(X\circ B=(X\ominus B)\oplus B\): removes small bright objects, keeps the shape of large ones;
- *closing* \(X\bullet B=(X\oplus B)\ominus B\): fills small dark holes and gaps.
Opening and closing are idempotent; *morphological profiles* (openings and closings with growing \(B\)) describe the size of structures around each pixel and are strong spatial features for classifying very high resolution images.

* Example
#+begin_src python
  from scipy import ndimage
  water = nir < T                                         # noisy threshold mask
  # drop isolated pixels
  clean = ndimage.binary_opening(water, structure=np.ones((3, 3)))
  # fill gaps in the river
  clean = ndimage.binary_closing(clean, structure=np.ones((5, 5)))
#+end_src

* Intuition
Roll a coin of the size of \(B\) inside the objects (opening) or outside them (closing): whatever the coin cannot reach is erased or filled.

* Pitfall
The structuring element encodes a scale and a shape: a square \(B\) removes a one-pixel-wide river together with the noise. Choose \(B\) smaller than the narrowest structure to keep.
