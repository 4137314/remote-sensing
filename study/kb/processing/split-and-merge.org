#+title: Split and merge segmentation
#+summary: Split: starting from the whole image, recursively divide every square block that fails the homogeneity test into four, building a quadtree whose leaves are homogeneous "atomic" regions; merge: join adjacent regions whose union is still homogeneous, removing the artificial square boundaries.

* Algorithm
#+CAPTION: Split and merge: split every block that fails the homogeneity predicate \(P\) into quadrants, then merge adjacent regions whose union satisfies it.
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Function{Split}{$B$}
\If{$P(B)$ true or $B$ is a single pixel} \State \Return $\{B\}$ \EndIf
\State divide $B$ into four quadrants $B_1,\dots,B_4$
\State \Return $\bigcup_{q}$ \Call{Split}{$B_q$}
\EndFunction
\Function{Merge}{$\mathcal R$}
\Repeat
\ForAll{adjacent $R_i,R_j\in\mathcal R$}
\If{$P(R_i\cup R_j)$} \State replace $R_i,R_j$ by $R_i\cup R_j$ \EndIf
\EndFor
\Until{no merge happens}
\State \Return $\mathcal R$
\EndFunction
\end{algorithmic}
\(P\): homogeneity predicate, e.g. \(\max-\min<\delta\) or variance below a threshold (optical), coefficient of variation compatible with speckle (SAR).

* Intuition
Zoom in only where the image is busy; then glue back together the pieces that turned out to be the same thing.

* Pitfall
The result depends on the order of merges and on block alignment: a region straddling a quadtree boundary is split early and must be recovered by merging.
