#+title: Contrast stretching
#+summary: Radiometric enhancement maps the occupied DN range onto the full display range: linear stretching \(\mathrm{GL}=255(\mathrm{DN}-\mathrm{DN}_{min})/(\mathrm{DN}_{max}-\mathrm{DN}_{min})\), linear with saturation at percentiles to ignore outliers, piecewise linear, or nonlinear (logarithmic, exponential) to expand dark or bright tones.

* Method
- *Linear*: \(\mathrm{GL}=a\,\mathrm{DN}+b\), with \(a=255/(\mathrm{DN}_{max}-\mathrm{DN}_{min})\), \(b=-a\,\mathrm{DN}_{min}\): a translation and a scaling of the histogram.
- *Linear with saturation*: \(\mathrm{DN}_{min},\mathrm{DN}_{max}\) taken at percentiles (e.g. 2% and 98%); values outside are clipped to 0 or 255. A few very bright pixels (clouds, metal roofs) no longer waste the range.
- *Piecewise linear*: different slopes for different DN intervals, to favour the interval of interest (water vs land).
- *Nonlinear*: \(\mathrm{GL}=c\log(1+\mathrm{DN})\) expands dark tones (useful for SAR amplitude); power laws (gamma) or exponentials expand bright tones; [[id:histogram-equalization][equalisation]] is the data-driven nonlinear case.

* Example
#+begin_src python
  lo, hi = np.percentile(band, [2, 98])
  gl = np.clip(255 * (band - lo) / (hi - lo), 0, 255).astype(np.uint8)
#+end_src

* Intuition
Stretching spends the display's gray levels where the data are, instead of on empty parts of the range.

* Pitfall
Stretching each band separately for a colour composite changes colour balance between images: two composites stretched independently cannot be compared by colour.
