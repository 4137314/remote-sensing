#+title: Image histogram
#+summary: The histogram \(h(k)\) counts, normalised by the number of pixels, how often each DN \(k\) occurs; it approximates the probability density of the image values and ignores where pixels are; its running sum, the cumulative histogram, approximates the distribution function.

* Definition
For an image of \(NM\) pixels:
\[ h(k)=\frac{\#\{(y,x):I(y,x)=k\}}{NM},\qquad \sum_k h(k)=1,\qquad H(k)=\sum_{j\le k}h(j). \]
\(H\) is non-decreasing and reaches 1. \(h\) is associated with the probability density of the DNs, \(H\) with the cumulative distribution, an approximation since DNs are discrete.

A bimodal histogram: two populations of pixels, e.g. water and land.

* Uses
- choosing stretch limits and LUTs;
- [[id:histogram-equalization][equalisation]] through \(H\);
- thresholds between modes for [[id:image-thresholding][segmentation]];
- detecting saturation (peaks at 0 or \(2^Q-1\)) and quantization gaps.

* Intuition
The histogram is the image with its geometry thrown away: it tells what values occur and how often, not where.

* Pitfall
Two very different images can have the same histogram (shuffle the pixels): histogram-based operations are blind to spatial structure.
