#+title: Statistics of homogeneous areas in optical images
#+summary: In images from passive sensors, the pixels of a physically homogeneous area still vary, because of detector thermal noise and natural variability of the spectral signature; many small independent effects add up, so the DNs of a class are well modelled by a Gaussian, multivariate across bands.

* Model
For a homogeneous region (e.g. one crop field), pixel values in band \(b\): \(x=\mu_b+n_{th}+n_{nat}\), sum of the class mean, thermal noise and natural variability (moisture, leaf angle, local slope). By the central limit theorem
\[ p(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma}\exp\!\Big(-\frac{(x-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}\Big), \]
and for a pixel vector \(\mathbf x\in\mathbb R^B\)
\[ p(\mathbf x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{B/2}|\Sigma|^{1/2}}\exp\!\Big(-\tfrac12(\mathbf x-\boldsymbol\mu)^\top\Sigma^{-1}(\mathbf x-\boldsymbol\mu)\Big), \]
with covariance \(\Sigma\) capturing the correlation between bands. Noise is *additive*: its variance does not depend on the mean level.

* Intuition
A field looks uniform to the eye and grainy to the sensor; the grain is many small random effects, and their sum is bell-shaped.

* Pitfall
Real classes are often not one Gaussian (a "forest" mixes species and slopes): multimodal classes need several spectral subclasses or non-parametric classifiers.
