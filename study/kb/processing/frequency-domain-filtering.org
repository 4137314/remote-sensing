#+title: Filtering in the frequency domain
#+summary: Multiplying the DFT by a transfer function \(H(k_y,k_x)\) and transforming back filters the image: a low-pass filter keeps smooth variations (blur), a high-pass filter keeps details and edges, a notch filter removes periodic noise; multiplication in frequency is convolution in space.

* Method
\[ I_{out}=\mathrm{IDFT}\big\{H\cdot\mathrm{DFT}\{I\}\big\},\qquad H\cdot I_F\ \leftrightarrow\ h\ast I. \]
With \(D=\sqrt{k_x^2+k_y^2}\) the distance from the (centred) origin and cut-off \(D_0\):
- *ideal low-pass*: \(H=1\) if \(D\le D_0\), else 0; *ideal high-pass*: \(1-H_{LP}\);
- *Gaussian*: \(H=e^{-D^2/2D_0^2}\), no ringing;
- *notch / band-stop*: zero small neighbourhoods around the peaks of periodic noise (striping).
Every image equals its low-pass plus its high-pass part.

* Example
#+begin_src python
  F = np.fft.fftshift(np.fft.fft2(band))
  ky, kx = np.indices(band.shape); D = np.hypot(ky - band.shape[0] / 2, kx - band.shape[1] / 2)
  H = np.exp(-D**2 / (2 * 30**2))
  low = np.real(np.fft.ifft2(np.fft.ifftshift(H * F)))
  high = band - low
#+end_src

* Intuition
Decide which stripe sizes to keep: big stripes are the landscape, small ones are the details and the noise.

* Pitfall
The sharp cut of the ideal filter produces ringing (ripples near edges), because its spatial kernel is a sinc; smooth transfer functions avoid it.
