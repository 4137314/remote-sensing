#+title: The convolution theorem
#+summary: Convolving an image with a kernel equals multiplying their Fourier transforms: \(\mathrm{DFT}\{h\ast I\}=H\cdot I_F\); so every moving-window filter has a transfer function telling which spatial frequencies it passes, and large kernels are cheaper through the FFT (\(O(NM\log NM)\) instead of \(O(NMK)\)).

* Statement
For \(N\times M\) arrays with circular (periodic) [[id:moving-window-filters][convolution]]
\[ (h\circledast I)(y,x)=\sum_{i,j}h(i,j)\,I(y-i,x-j)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \mathrm{DFT}\{h\circledast I\}=H(k_y,k_x)\,I_F(k_y,k_x). \]
Dually, multiplication in space is convolution in frequency.

* Derivation
Substitute the convolution into the DFT and change variables \(y'=y-i\), \(x'=x-j\):
\[ \sum_{y,x}\sum_{i,j}h(i,j)I(y-i,x-j)e^{-j2\pi(\frac{k_yy}{N}+\frac{k_xx}{M})}=\sum_{i,j}h(i,j)e^{-j2\pi(\frac{k_yi}{N}+\frac{k_xj}{M})}\sum_{y',x'}I(y',x')e^{-j2\pi(\frac{k_yy'}{N}+\frac{k_xx'}{M})}, \]
using periodicity to keep the sums over a full period.

* Example
The \(1\times3\) mean filter \(h=(1,1,1)/3\) has \(H(k)=\frac13\big(1+2\cos(2\pi k/M)\big)\): 1 at \(k=0\) (mean preserved), zero at \(k=M/3\), negative beyond — a poor low-pass with sign inversions, which is why Gaussian kernels are preferred. The PSF of a sensor acts the same way: its transform, the MTF, is the transfer function of the acquisition.

* Intuition
A filter is a set of volume knobs, one per [[id:discrete-fourier-transform][spatial frequency]]; convolving in space and turning the knobs in frequency are the same operation.

* Pitfall
The DFT implies periodic images: filtering through the FFT wraps the right edge onto the left. Pad the image (with zeros or mirrored borders) before transforming.
