#+title: Image segmentation
#+summary: Segmentation partitions the image into connected regions that are homogeneous by some criterion and labels each region; the criterion depends on the data (for optical images closeness of means, for SAR closeness of mean intensities given multiplicative speckle), and the main families are histogram thresholding, contour-based, split-and-merge and region growing.

* Definition
A segmentation is a partition \(\{R_1,\dots,R_n\}\) of the pixels with
1. \(\bigcup_i R_i=\) image, \(R_i\cap R_j=\emptyset\);
2. each \(R_i\) connected;
3. \(P(R_i)=\) true for a homogeneity predicate \(P\);
4. \(P(R_i\cup R_j)=\) false for adjacent \(R_i,R_j\).
Homogeneity criteria: optical — region [[id:image-statistics][variance]] below a threshold, or means of adjacent regions within a tolerance; SAR — ratio of mean intensities close to 1, coefficient of variation compatible with speckle.

Methods:
- [[id:image-thresholding][histogram thresholding]];
- contour-based ([[id:edge-detection][edges]] closed into boundaries);
- [[id:split-and-merge][split and merge]];
- [[id:region-growing][region growing]].

* Uses
[[id:feature-extraction][Region-based]] classification (object-based image analysis): classify regions instead of pixels, using shape, size and texture features; reduces salt-and-pepper noise in maps, especially in very high resolution images where single pixels are parts of objects.

* Intuition
Segmentation turns pixels into objects — fields, lakes, buildings — before anyone asks what the objects are.

* Pitfall
There is no single correct segmentation: the scale (minimum region size, homogeneity tolerance) decides whether a field is one region or a dozen, and the right scale depends on the application.
