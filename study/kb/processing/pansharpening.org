#+title: Pansharpening
#+summary: Sensors pair a fine panchromatic band with coarser multispectral bands; pansharpening injects the spatial detail of the pan band into the co-registered, upsampled multispectral image — by component substitution (IHS, PCA, Gram–Schmidt) or by adding the pan's high-pass detail — trading some spectral fidelity for resolution.

* Method
Co-register and resample the multispectral bands \(M_b\) to the pan grid.
- *Brovey*: \(\hat M_b=M_b\,\dfrac{P}{\frac1B\sum_kM_k}\): each band rescaled by the ratio of pan to mean intensity.
- *IHS substitution* (3 bands): transform RGB to intensity–hue–saturation, replace \(I\) with the pan (histogram-matched), transform back. Equivalent to \(\hat M_b=M_b+(P-I)\).
- *PCA / Gram–Schmidt*: replace the first component (or a synthetic low-resolution pan) with the pan.
- *High-pass injection*: \(\hat M_b=M_b+g_b\,(P-P_{LP})\), where \(P_{LP}\) is the pan blurred to the multispectral resolution: only detail is added, preserving the spectra.

* Example
WorldView-3: pan 0.31 m, multispectral 1.24 m: pansharpened RGB at 0.31 m. Quality is assessed spectrally (spectral angle, ERGAS against degraded references) and spatially (correlation of high-pass details with the pan).

* Intuition
Take the colours from the blurry photo and the sharpness from the black-and-white one.

* Pitfall
The pan band's spectral range does not match the sum of the multispectral bands (it often extends into the NIR): substitution methods then distort colours, especially of vegetation. Sharpened products are for viewing and object delineation; radiometric analysis should use the original bands.
