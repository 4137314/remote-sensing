#+title: Two-dimensional discrete Fourier transform
#+summary: The 2-D DFT writes an \(N\times M\) image as a sum of sinusoidal patterns, \(I_F(k_y,k_x)=\sum_{y,x}I(y,x)\,e^{-j2\pi(k_yy/N+k_xx/M)}\); it is periodic, Hermitian-symmetric for real images, its value at the origin is \(NM\) times the mean, low frequencies hold smooth variations and high frequencies edges and details.

* Definition
1-D: \(D(k)=\sum_{x=0}^{M-1}d(x)e^{-j2\pi kx/M}\), \(d(x)=\frac1M\sum_k D(k)e^{j2\pi kx/M}\), periodic with period \(M\); the largest representable frequency (Nyquist) is \(k=M/2\).
2-D:
\[ I_F(k_y,k_x)=\sum_{y=0}^{N-1}\sum_{x=0}^{M-1}I(y,x)\,e^{-j2\pi\left(\frac{k_yy}{N}+\frac{k_xx}{M}\right)}, \]
\[ I(y,x)=\frac{1}{NM}\sum_{k_y,k_x}I_F(k_y,k_x)\,e^{j2\pi\left(\frac{k_yy}{N}+\frac{k_xx}{M}\right)}. \]
Properties: separable (rows then columns, \(O(NM\log NM)\) with the FFT); periodic in both indices (the image is implicitly tiled); \(I_F(0,0)=NM\,\mu\); for real images \(I_F(-k_y,-k_x)=I_F^*(k_y,k_x)\). Usually displayed as \(\log(1+|I_F|)\), shifted so the origin is centred.

* Spatial structures
- periodic patterns (field rows, waves) give isolated peaks at their frequency, perpendicular to their stripes;
- straight linear features (roads, edges) give a line through the origin perpendicular to them;
- irregular natural textures give diffuse spectra.

* Example
#+begin_src python
  F = np.fft.fftshift(np.fft.fft2(band))
  plt.imshow(np.log1p(np.abs(F)), cmap="gray")
#+end_src

* Intuition
The DFT re-expresses the image as a recipe of stripes: how much of each stripe width and orientation it contains.

* Pitfall
The magnitude alone does not determine the image: the phase carries the positions of the structures, and swapping the phases of two images swaps what they look like.
