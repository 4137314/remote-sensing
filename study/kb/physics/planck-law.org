#+title: Black body and Planck's law
#+summary: A black body absorbs all incident radiation and emits, at temperature \(T\), the spectral exitance \(B(\lambda,T)=\dfrac{2\pi hc^2\lambda^{-5}}{e^{hc/\lambda kT}-1}\); every body above 0 K emits, and real bodies are described relative to this reference.

* Statement
Every body at \(T>0\) K emits electromagnetic radiation depending on \(T\) and on its surface, and reflects, absorbs or transmits radiation from outside. The *black body* is the ideal emitter and absorber:
\[ B(\lambda,T)=\frac{2\pi c^2h\,\lambda^{-5}}{e^{\,ch/(\lambda kT)}-1}=\frac{c_1\lambda^{-5}}{e^{\,c_2/(\lambda T)}-1}\quad[\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}\,\mu m^{-1}}], \]
with \(c=2.99\times10^8\) m s\(^{-1}\), \(h=6.62\times10^{-34}\) J s, \(k=1.38\times10^{-23}\) J K\(^{-1}\), \(c_1=2\pi c^2h\), \(c_2=hc/k\).

* Consequences
- the area under the curve grows as \(T^4\) and the peak moves to shorter \(\lambda\) as \(T\) grows ([[id:wien-stefan-boltzmann][Stefan–Boltzmann and Wien]]);
- curves for different \(T\) never cross: at every \(\lambda\) a hotter body emits more;
- for \(\lambda T\gg c_2\) (microwaves) \(B\approx c_1\lambda^{-4}T/c_2\): radiance proportional to temperature (Rayleigh–Jeans), the basis of passive [[id:electromagnetic-spectrum][microwave]] radiometry.

* Worked example
Total solar exitance: \(\sigma T^4=5.67\times10^{-8}\cdot5778^4\approx6.3\times10^7\) W m\(^{-2}\). At the Earth this is diluted by \((R_\odot/d)^2=(6.96\times10^8/1.496\times10^{11})^2\approx2.16\times10^{-5}\): \(\approx1360\) W m\(^{-2}\), the solar constant. The Earth at 288 K emits \(\sigma T^4\approx390\) W m\(^{-2}\), mostly beyond 4 µm.

* Intuition
Temperature fixes both how much a body glows and in what colour: a stove plate goes from invisible infrared to red to orange as it heats.

* Pitfall
Planck's law describes emission only. In the reflective bands what the sensor sees is reflected sunlight, governed by reflectance, not by the temperature of the ground.
