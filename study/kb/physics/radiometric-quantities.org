#+title: Radiometric quantities
#+summary: Radiant energy [J], flux [W], exitance and irradiance (flux leaving or reaching a surface per unit area, W m\(^{-2}\)), intensity (flux per solid angle, W sr\(^{-1}\)) and radiance (flux per unit projected area and solid angle, W m\(^{-2}\) sr\(^{-1}\)): radiance is what a sensor measures, and it does not change with distance in a lossless medium.

* Definition
| quantity | symbol | unit | meaning |
|---+---+---+---|
| radiant energy | \(Q\) | J | energy of the EM wave |
| radiant flux | \(\Phi=dQ/dt\) | W | power emitted, reflected, transmitted or received |
| exitance | \(M=d\Phi/dA\) | W m\(^{-2}\) | flux emitted per unit area |
| irradiance | \(E=d\Phi/dA\) | W m\(^{-2}\) | flux received per unit area |
| intensity | \(I=d\Phi/d\Omega\) | W sr\(^{-1}\) | flux per unit solid angle |
| radiance | \(L=\dfrac{d^2\Phi}{dA\cos\theta\, d\Omega}\) | W m\(^{-2}\) sr\(^{-1}\) | flux per projected area and solid angle |

Spectral versions are per unit wavelength (W m\(^{-2}\) sr\(^{-1}\) µm\(^{-1}\)). *Reflectance* \(\rho=E_r/E_i\) is a dimensionless ratio, the physical property of the surface that the [[id:spectral-signature][spectral signature]] describes.

* Derivation
A detector with area \(A_d\) behind optics of solid angle \(\Omega\) collects \(\Phi\approx L\,A_d\,\Omega\). Moving the sensor away from a uniform surface, the ground area seen grows as \(R^2\) while the solid angle of each ground element shrinks as \(R^{-2}\): the collected flux, hence the measured radiance, does not depend on distance.

* Intuition
Irradiance is how much light falls on a surface; radiance is how bright that surface looks from a given direction — the quantity a camera pixel records.

* Pitfall
Radiance depends on the viewing and illumination geometry; reflectance does not (for a Lambertian surface). Comparing images taken at different sun angles requires converting radiance to reflectance.
