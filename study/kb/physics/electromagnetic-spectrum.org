#+title: Electromagnetic spectrum and spectral regions
#+summary: Radiation is ordered by wavelength \(\lambda=c/f\); remote sensing of the Earth uses the visible (0.4–0.7 µm), near and short-wave infrared (reflected sunlight), mid and thermal infrared (emitted heat, 3–14 µm) and microwaves (1 mm–1 m, radar and radiometers), the regions where the atmosphere is transparent.

* Definition
\(\lambda f=c\), \(c\approx3\times10^8\) m/s; photon energy \(E=hf\). From short to long: gamma rays, X rays, ultraviolet, visible (380–750 nm), infrared, microwaves, radio waves. No source or sensor covers the whole range.

| region | \(\lambda\) (µm) | source | surface property |
|---+---+---+---|
| Visible (V) | 0.4–0.7 | Sun | reflectance |
| Near infrared (NIR) | 0.7–1.1 | Sun | reflectance |
| Short-wave IR (SWIR) | 1.1–1.35, 1.4–1.8, 2–2.5 | Sun | reflectance |
| Mid-wave IR (MWIR) | 3–4, 4.5–5 | Sun and Earth | reflectance and temperature |
| Thermal IR (TIR) | 8–9.5, 10–14 | Earth | temperature, emissivity |
| Microwave | \(10^3\)–\(10^6\) | Earth (passive), sensor (active) | temperature, roughness, moisture |

The gaps between the intervals are absorption bands of water vapour and CO\(_2\): see [[id:atmospheric-windows][atmospheric windows]].

* Intuition
Each region answers a different question: colour and vegetation in the reflective bands, temperature in the thermal ones, geometry and moisture in the microwaves.

* Pitfall
Bands are named differently by different missions (Landsat "SWIR1", Sentinel-2 "B11"); always reason with the wavelength interval, not the label.
