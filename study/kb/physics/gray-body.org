#+title: Real bodies: emissivity and Kirchhoff's law
#+summary: Incident energy splits into reflected, absorbed and transmitted parts, \(\rho(\lambda)+\alpha(\lambda)+\tau(\lambda)=1\); a real body emits \(\varepsilon(\lambda)B(\lambda,T)\) with emissivity \(\varepsilon<1\), and Kirchhoff's law \(\alpha=\varepsilon\) gives \(\rho+\tau+\varepsilon=1\): good absorbers are good emitters.

* Statement
For a surface hit by \(E_i(\lambda)\): \(E_r+E_a+E_t=E_i\), so with \(\rho=E_r/E_i\), \(\alpha=E_a/E_i\), \(\tau=E_t/E_i\)
\[ \rho(\lambda)+\alpha(\lambda)+\tau(\lambda)=1. \]
Emissivity \(\varepsilon(\lambda)=B_{real}(\lambda,T)/B_{bb}(\lambda,T)\), \(0\le\varepsilon<1\). Kirchhoff (1860): in thermal equilibrium \(\alpha(\lambda)=\varepsilon(\lambda)\), hence \(\rho+\tau+\varepsilon=1\), and for opaque bodies (\(\tau=0\)) \(\rho=1-\varepsilon\).
- black body: \(\varepsilon=1\), \(\rho=0\);
- perfect reflector: \(\varepsilon=0\), \(\rho=1\).
A *gray body* has \(\varepsilon\) constant in \(\lambda\): \(B=\varepsilon B_{bb}\), \(M=\bar\varepsilon\sigma T^4\).

* Example
Water has \(\varepsilon\approx0.98\) in the thermal infrared: it reflects almost nothing there and looks like a black body. Polished metal has \(\varepsilon\approx0.05\): it looks cold to a thermal camera while reflecting the sky.

* Intuition
Energy that is not reflected or transmitted is absorbed, and whatever absorbs a wavelength well also emits it well.

* Pitfall
A thermal sensor measures \(\varepsilon B(T)\), not \(T\): two surfaces at the same temperature with different emissivities give different radiances. Retrieving temperature requires knowing \(\varepsilon\) (brightness temperature vs kinetic temperature).
