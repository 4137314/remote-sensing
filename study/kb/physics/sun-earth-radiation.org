#+title: The Sun and the Earth as sources
#+summary: Modelled as black bodies, the Sun (5778 K) peaks at 0.5 µm and dominates the visible to short-wave infrared, the Earth (300 K) peaks at 9.66 µm in the thermal infrared; the two curves cross near 3–5 µm, so reflective sensing and thermal sensing use different bands.

* Example
By [[id:wien-stefan-boltzmann][Wien's law]]:
\[ \lambda_{\max,\odot}=\frac{2898}{5778}\approx0.50\ \mu\mathrm m\ (\text{visible}), \]
\[ \lambda_{\max,\oplus}=\frac{2898}{300}\approx9.66\ \mu\mathrm m\ (\text{thermal IR}). \]
Materials on the Earth absorb, reflect or transmit solar energy between about 0.4 and 3 µm. Reaching the Earth, the solar [[id:radiometric-quantities][irradiance]] is diluted by the factor \((R_\odot/d)^2\approx2.2\times10^{-5}\), so in the 3–5 µm region reflected sunlight and Earth emission are comparable; above 5 µm emission dominates.

Sunlight reaching the ground versus Earth emission: they cross between 3 and 5 µm.

* Intuition
By day, below 3 µm we see the Sun's light bounced off the ground; above 5 µm we see the ground's own warmth, day and night.

* Pitfall
At night the reflective bands are blind, but thermal bands still work: night images are thermal or radar images.
