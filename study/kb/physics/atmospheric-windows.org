#+title: Atmospheric transmittance and windows
#+summary: The atmosphere transmits a fraction \(\tau_a(\lambda)=E_t/E_i\) of the radiation crossing it; water vapour and CO\(_2\) absorb strongly in some bands (e.g. 1.4 and 1.9 µm, 2.7 µm, 6 µm), so sensors are designed in the windows where \(\tau_a\) is high: visible–NIR, SWIR, 3–5 µm, 8–14 µm and microwaves.

* Definition
\[ \tau_a(\lambda)=\frac{E_t(\lambda)}{E_i(\lambda)} \]
for radiation crossing the atmosphere, on the way down (Sun–ground) and on the way up (ground–sensor). Low-transmittance regions are absorption bands of H\(_2\)O, CO\(_2\), O\(_3\), O\(_2\). \(\tau_a\) also varies with weather: clouds bring it to almost zero in the visible and infrared, while microwaves (except near the 1.36 cm water-vapour line) cross clouds, fog and rain.

* Consequences for design
1. place bands inside windows, avoiding absorption features;
2. pick acquisition conditions (season, time, cloud cover);
3. still separate the atmospheric contribution from the surface one: [[id:radiometric-correction][atmospheric correction]].

* Intuition
The atmosphere is a coloured filter between the ground and the sensor; we can only look through the clear parts of the glass.

* Pitfall
Absorption bands are not useless: sensors for the atmosphere itself (water vapour, ozone, CO\(_2\)) work exactly in them.
