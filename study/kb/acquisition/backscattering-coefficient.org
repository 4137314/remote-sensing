#+title: Backscattering coefficient
#+summary: \(\sigma^0\) is the radar cross section per unit area, usually in dB; it depends on the radar (wavelength, polarization, incidence angle) and on the surface (roughness relative to \(\lambda\), dielectric constant i.e. moisture, geometry and orientation): smooth surfaces mirror energy away and look dark, rough ones and corners look bright.

* Definition
\(\sigma^0=\sigma/A\), dimensionless, expressed as \(10\log_{10}\sigma^0\) dB (typically −25 to +5 dB).

System parameters:
- *wavelength*: longer waves penetrate deeper into vegetation and dry soil (X: canopy top; C: canopy; L and P: branches, trunks, ground);
- *polarization*: transmit and receive horizontally (H) or vertically (V): HH, VV co-polarized; HV, VH cross-polarized, sensitive to volume scattering (vegetation);
- *incidence angle* \(\theta\): \(\sigma^0\) decreases with \(\theta\), fast for smooth surfaces.
Surface parameters:
- *roughness*: smooth if height variations \(h<\lambda/(8\cos\theta)\) (Rayleigh criterion), giving specular reflection away from the sensor (calm water: black);
- *dielectric constant*: water raises it, so wet soil and vegetation backscatter more;
- *geometry*: slopes facing the radar are bright, dihedral corners (building–ground) give strong double-bounce returns.

* Intuition
A radar image is a map of roughness, wetness and geometry seen from the side, not a map of colour.

* Pitfall
The same \(\sigma^0\) can come from different causes (a wet smooth field and a dry rough one): interpretation needs several bands, polarizations, angles or dates.
