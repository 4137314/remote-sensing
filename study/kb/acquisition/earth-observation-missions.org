#+title: Main Earth-observation missions
#+summary: Geostationary meteorological satellites (Meteosat) trade resolution for continuous coverage; the Landsat series gives the longest optical record at 30 m; SPOT, IKONOS, QuickBird and WorldView push to sub-metre commercial imaging; PRISMA brings hyperspectral data; ERS, ENVISAT, COSMO-SkyMed, TerraSAR-X and Biomass cover SAR from C to X to P band.

* Example
| mission              | type          | key data                                 |
|----------------------+---------------+------------------------------------------|
| Meteosat SG (2002)   | GEO           | SEVIRI, 12 channels, 3 km, every 15 min  |
| Landsat 8 / 9        | optical       | OLI 9 bands, 30 m (pan 15 m); TIRS 100 m |
| SPOT 6 / 7           | optical       | pushbroom, 1.5 m pan, 6 m multispectral  |
| IKONOS, QuickBird    | optical VHR   | first commercial sub-metre (1 m, 0.6 m)  |
| WorldView-3 / 4      | optical VHR   | 0.31 m pan, 8 VNIR + 8 SWIR bands        |
| PRISMA (2019)        | hyperspectral | ≈ 240 bands, 0.4–2.5 μm, 30 m            |
| ERS-1 / 2            | SAR           | C band VV, ≈ 25 m; GOME ozone (ERS-2)    |
| ENVISAT (2002–12)    | multi-sensor  | ASAR (C band), MERIS, AATSR              |
| COSMO-SkyMed         | SAR           | X band, 1 m spotlight, revisit in hours  |
| TerraSAR-X, TanDEM-X | SAR pair      | X band, 1 m; InSAR global DEM            |
| Biomass (2025)       | SAR           | first P band in space: forest biomass    |

* Trends
[[id:sensor-resolutions][Geometric resolution]] of passive systems went from ~80 m (Landsat 1) to 0.3 m (WorldView) in fifty years; constellations of small satellites (PlanetScope, Capella, Umbra) trade size for daily or sub-daily revisit.

* Intuition
No single mission does everything: missions specialise in revisit, resolution, spectral detail or wavelength, and [[id:remote-sensing-applications][applications]] combine them.

* Pitfall
Comparing data across missions requires harmonising bands, resolutions, calibration and acquisition times; long time series (Landsat since 1972) are valuable precisely because they were kept consistent.
