#+title: Synthetic aperture radar (SAR)
#+summary: A small antenna moving along the track illuminates each target during a whole stretch of path \(L_s=\lambda R/L\); recording amplitude and phase of all echoes and combining them coherently synthesizes an antenna of length \(L_s\), whose resolution is \(r_A=L/2\): finer for smaller antennas and independent of range and wavelength.

* Construction
A target T is in the beam while the antenna travels from A to B, a distance equal to the real beam footprint \(L_s=\lambda R/L\): this is the synthetic antenna. During that time its range \(R(t)\) varies quadratically and the echo phase \(4\pi R(t)/\lambda\) gives a Doppler history (a chirp in azimuth) unique to the target's along-track position. Correlating with the expected history (azimuth compression, the azimuth analogue of [[id:chirp-pulse-compression][pulse compression]]) focuses the target. A synthetic antenna of length \(L_s\) with two-way phase has beamwidth \(\lambda/(2L_s)\), so
\[ r_A=\frac{\lambda}{2L_s}\,R=\frac{\lambda R}{2}\cdot\frac{L}{\lambda R}=\frac{L}{2}. \]

T is seen from every antenna position between A and B.

* Consequences
- resolution independent of range: the farther the target, the longer the synthetic antenna;
- smaller antennas give finer resolution, but need higher pulse repetition frequency and give narrower swaths (ambiguity constraints);
- both amplitude and phase must be recorded: the product is a [[id:sar-complex-image][complex image]];
- modes: stripmap, spotlight (beam steered to stay on a target: longer \(L_s\), finer resolution), ScanSAR/TOPS (bursts across sub-swaths: wide swath, coarser resolution).

* Intuition
Instead of one huge antenna, take many snapshots with a small one while moving, and add them up as if they had been taken at once.

* Pitfall
Coherent focusing assumes a still scene: moving targets (ships, cars) are displaced in azimuth or blurred ("train off the tracks").
