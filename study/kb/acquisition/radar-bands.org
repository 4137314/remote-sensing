#+title: Radar frequency bands
#+summary: Imaging radars use letter-named microwave bands: Ka (26.5–40 GHz), K, Ku (12.5–18), X (8–12.5, COSMO-SkyMed, TerraSAR-X), C (4–8, Sentinel-1), S (2–4, NISAR), L (1–2, SMAP, NISAR), P (0.3–1 GHz, Biomass); longer wavelengths penetrate further into vegetation, soil and ice.

* Definition
| band | frequency (GHz) | λ (cm)  | example systems                      |
|------+-----------------+---------+--------------------------------------|
| Ka   | 26.5–40         | 0.8–1.1 | SSM/I (37), SARAL (35.75)            |
| K    | 18–26.5         | 1.1–1.7 | SSM/I (19.35, 22.235)                |
| Ku   | 12.5–18         | 1.7–2.4 | RapidScat (13.4), Cassini (13.8)     |
| X    | 8–12.5          | 2.4–3.8 | COSMO-SkyMed, TerraSAR-X (9.6)       |
| C    | 4–8             | 3.8–7.5 | Sentinel-1 (5.4), RISAT-1            |
| S    | 2–4             | 7.5–15  | Magellan (2.385), NISAR (3.2)        |
| L    | 1–2             | 15–30   | SMAP (1.22), SIR-A/B/C, NISAR (1.25) |
| P    | 0.3–1           | 30–100  | Biomass (0.435)                      |

* Choosing a band
Penetration grows with \(\lambda\): X sees the top of the canopy and is good for urban detail and fine resolution; C is a robust all-round choice ([[id:remote-sensing-applications][agriculture]], sea ice, oil spills, [[id:sar-interferometry][interferometry]]); L reaches branches and ground, keeping coherence over vegetation; P measures forest [[id:earth-observation-missions][biomass]] and sees under dry sand and ice. Water vapour absorbs near 22 GHz (1.36 cm), which radiometers use and imaging radars avoid.

* Worked example
Antenna size for a given beamwidth scales with \(\lambda\): an azimuth beamwidth of 3° (\(5.2\times10^{-2}\) rad) needs \(L=\lambda/\beta\): 0.6 m at X band (3.1 cm), 1.1 m at C band (5.6 cm), 4.6 m at L band (24 cm), 13 m at P band (69 cm) — which is why Biomass carries a 12 m deployable reflector.

* Intuition
The wavelength is the radar's yardstick: it notices structures of its own size and looks through what is much smaller.

* Pitfall
Longer wavelengths need larger antennas for the same beam width and suffer ionospheric effects (Faraday rotation) at L and P band.
