#+title: Side-looking radar geometry
#+summary: An imaging radar looks sideways, perpendicular to its track: points are separated in range by the delay of their echoes and in azimuth by the motion of the platform; looking straight down would be ambiguous, because points symmetric about the track return at the same time.

* Definition
- *Azimuth*: the flight direction (along track).
- *Range*: across track. *Slant range* \(R\) is the distance along the line of sight; *ground range* its projection on the ground.
- *Near range / far range*: the edges of the swath closest to and farthest from the track.
- *Look angle* (at the antenna) and *incidence angle* \(\theta\) (at the ground, between the beam and the local normal) change across the swath.
Hardware: transmitter, duplexer (switches the antenna between transmit and receive), receiver, antenna.

* Why side-looking
Range separation needs different delays. With a nadir-looking radar two points A and B at equal distance on the two sides of the track return simultaneously: ambiguity. Illuminating only one side removes it; points at different ground ranges have different slant ranges.

* Intuition
The radar measures distances, not angles: the image is a map of "how far" against "when along the track".

* Pitfall
In slant-range geometry the ground is compressed at near range relative to far range; images must be projected to ground range before measuring distances.
