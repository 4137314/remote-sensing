#+title: Detector, quantization and digital numbers
#+summary: The detector converts the radiance focused by the optics into a current or voltage, which is sampled and quantized with \(Q\) bits into a digital number \(\mathrm{DN}\in\{0,\dots,2^Q-1\}\); calibration relates DN linearly to radiance, \(L=\mathrm{gain}\cdot\mathrm{DN}+\mathrm{offset}\).

* Definition
Chain: mirror and optics collect radiance → filters or prism select the band → detector (transducer) converts EM energy variations into an electrical signal → amplifier → sampling and A/D conversion → DN. Each pixel's DN represents the radiance integrated over its ground cell, spectral band and integration time.

Linear quantization with \(Q\) bits over the range \([L_{min},L_{max}]\):
\[ \mathrm{DN}=\mathrm{round}\!\left((2^Q-1)\frac{L-L_{min}}{L_{max}-L_{min}}\right),\qquad L=\mathrm{gain}\cdot\mathrm{DN}+\mathrm{offset}. \]
Gains and offsets come from pre-launch and on-board calibration (lamps, solar diffusers, deep space views) and are distributed in the image metadata.

* Example
[[id:earth-observation-missions][Landsat]] 8 OLI quantizes at 12 bits (4096 levels, delivered in 16-bit words); [[id:copernicus-sentinel][Sentinel-2]] delivers 16-bit products; older sensors used 8 bits (256 levels).

* Intuition
A DN is a measurement in arbitrary units: meaningful within one band of one image, comparable across images only after conversion to radiance or reflectance.

* Pitfall
More bits are useless if the noise is larger than one quantization step: \(Q\) should match the [[id:sensor-resolutions][radiometric resolution]], not exceed it.
