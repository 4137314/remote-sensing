#+title: Range resolution
#+summary: Two targets are separated in range if their echoes do not overlap: with a pulse of duration \(\tau\), slant-range resolution is \(r_{SR}=c\tau/2\) and ground-range resolution \(r_{GR}=c\tau/(2\sin\theta)\), finer at far range and independent of the distance from the sensor.

* Statement
\[ r_{SR}=\frac{c\,\tau}{2},\qquad r_{GR}=\frac{c\,\tau}{2\sin\theta}. \]

* Derivation
Targets A and B at slant ranges \(R\) and \(R+\Delta R\): B's echo arrives \(2\Delta R/c\) after A's. The two pulses of length \(\tau\) do not overlap if \(2\Delta R/c\ge\tau\), i.e. \(\Delta R\ge c\tau/2\). On the ground a horizontal distance \(\Delta x\) corresponds to \(\Delta R=\Delta x\sin\theta\), so \(\Delta x\ge c\tau/(2\sin\theta)\).

* Example
\(\tau=0.1\) µs gives \(r_{SR}=15\) m; at \(\theta=20^\circ\) \(r_{GR}\approx44\) m, at \(50^\circ\) about 20 m.

* Consequences
- resolution does not degrade with altitude: it depends only on the pulse;
- it degrades towards near range (small \(\theta\)) and is undefined at nadir (\(\theta=0\)), another reason to look sideways;
- a finer resolution needs a shorter pulse, hence higher peak power for the same energy — solved by [[id:chirp-pulse-compression][chirp pulses]].

* Intuition
Range resolution is half the pulse length in metres: echoes from points closer than that blur into one.

* Pitfall
Ground-range resolution varies across the swath: the pixel spacing of a ground-range product is constant, its true resolution is not.
