#+title: Point spread function and spectral response
#+summary: Real sensors do not integrate over ideal boxes: spatially the DN is the radiance weighted by the point spread function, which overlaps neighbouring cells and blurs edges; spectrally it is weighted by the relative spectral response of each band, a smooth curve instead of a rectangle.

* Definition
The DN of band \(b\) at pixel \((x_0,y_0)\) is, up to calibration,
\[ \mathrm{DN}_b(x_0,y_0)\propto\int\!\!\int\!\!\int L(x,y,\lambda)\,\mathrm{PSF}(x-x_0,y-y_0)\,R_b(\lambda)\,dx\,dy\,d\lambda, \]
with \(\mathrm{PSF}\) the spatial response (optics diffraction, detector size, image motion, electronics) and \(R_b\) the relative spectral response of band \(b\). Ideal integration would use rectangles; real ones are bell-shaped with tails. The [[id:discrete-fourier-transform][Fourier transform]] of the PSF is the modulation transfer function (MTF): how much contrast survives at each spatial frequency.

The real response reaches into the neighbouring pixels.

* Worked example
A Gaussian PSF with \(\sigma=0.42\) pixel has full width at half maximum \(2\sqrt{2\ln2}\,\sigma\approx1\) pixel. Its MTF \(e^{-2\pi^2\sigma^2f^2}\) at the Nyquist frequency \(f=0.5\) cycles/pixel is \(e^{-2\pi^2\cdot0.176\cdot0.25}\approx0.42\): patterns at the finest resolvable scale keep 42% of their contrast.

* Intuition
Every pixel is a little blurred mixture of its neighbours, and every band a little mixture of nearby colours.

* Pitfall
Small bright objects (a road, a boat) can be detected even if narrower than a pixel, while two objects one pixel apart may not be separated: detectability and resolution are different things.
