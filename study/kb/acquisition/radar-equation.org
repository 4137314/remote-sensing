#+title: Radar principle and radar equation
#+summary: A radar transmits a pulse and measures the echo: its delay gives the range \(R=c\,t/2\), its power \(P_r=\dfrac{P_tG^2\lambda^2\sigma}{(4\pi)^3R^4}\) depends on the radar cross section \(\sigma\) of the target, i.e. on how the surface backscatters at that wavelength.

* Statement
For a monostatic radar with transmitted power \(P_t\), antenna gain \(G\), wavelength \(\lambda\), target radar cross section \(\sigma\) (m\(^2\)) at range \(R\):
\[ P_r=\frac{P_t\,G^2\,\lambda^2\,\sigma}{(4\pi)^3\,R^4}. \]
Range from the round-trip time \(t\): \(R=ct/2\). For distributed targets (terrain) \(\sigma\) is replaced by \(\sigma^0A\), with \(\sigma^0\) the dimensionless [[id:backscattering-coefficient][backscattering coefficient]] and \(A\) the illuminated area.

* Derivation
Power density at the target: \(P_tG/(4\pi R^2)\). The target intercepts and re-radiates isotropically the power \(\sigma\,P_tG/(4\pi R^2)\); at the radar the density is that divided by \(4\pi R^2\); the antenna collects it over its effective area \(A_e=G\lambda^2/(4\pi)\). Multiplying gives the equation; the \(R^{-4}\) comes from the two spreadings.

* Uses
Radars measure two quantities: distance (altimeters, ranging) and backscattered power. Imaging radars for [[id:remote-sensing-system][Earth observation]] are interested mainly in the backscattering coefficient, which carries the physical information about the surface; they work in microwaves (1 mm–1 m), day and night, through clouds, fog and rain.

* Intuition
Shouting at a wall and listening: the delay tells how far it is, the loudness of the echo what it is made of.

* Pitfall
The \(R^{-4}\) law makes the echo extremely weak from orbit: spaceborne radars need high peak power, large antennas and the processing gain of [[id:chirp-pulse-compression][pulse compression]] and [[id:synthetic-aperture-radar][synthetic aperture]].
